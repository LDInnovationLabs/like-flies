# v19 playtest targets — Job and Sage

Acceptance targets prepared before the candidate. This is not a pass report. Final menu wording and implemented limits belong in the delivered build notes.

## Start with Buzz Tag

Use the same v19 build and room. Both choose Fly. One player joins Tag, then the second. The first should see a waiting message until the second opts in. Identify IT without having to explain which color means what.

Chase around the grill, through the open lawn and under a bench. Try a slow touch, a fast crossing, a near miss and a pair separated by a wall. A legal close pass should transfer IT exactly once. The new IT needs a visible escape countdown; the old IT needs enough time to get away before being tagged back. Park overlapping for a moment: that should not generate a stream of points.

Leave Tag. The remaining player should not be able to tag a spectator, bot or human. Switch roles and respawn. If a third person is available, leave them opted out and confirm they can race/explore while the other two play. Test the coordinator leaving only after basic chase feels understandable.

Creative questions: Is the reach too generous or frustratingly small? Is the chase fun in flight and on surfaces? Does the cooldown give a real escape? Can you tell who is IT from a comfortable distance? Does the activity create fly-to-fly stories worth repeating?

## Hide-a-Crumb

One player lands at a reachable static hiding spot and confirms a crumb. The other selects that challenge and searches from farther away. The scent clue should help exploration without drawing a straight line to the answer. A hidden crumb should not be findable through a wall. The creator should not get a find for their own hide.

After a find, the crumb should still exist for another seeker. Revisit it or reload: no duplicate reward. Have the hider leave while the seeker remains. Finally export a challenge code, import it in a fresh session and check that the same reachable location survives. “Room session” and “saved on this browser” must be labelled honestly.

Creative questions: Is hiding satisfying? Does the clue preserve a little mystery? Does it make you inspect places you normally fly past? Are any spots unfair to reach or see?

## Ghost Wings

Complete a valid race lap, then select your best compatible ghost and repeat from the same start ring. The ghost should start with the first accepted gate, follow the recorded line and finish at its recorded time. It must not tag you, block you, collect rings or alter your score. Off should remove it immediately.

Try a different start ring or tuning: the UI should explain compatibility instead of showing a misleading rival. Share your ghost explicitly, then let the other player request it. Without sharing, they should not receive the replay. A missing shared ghost must not erase the race record.

Creative questions: Does it teach the route? Does it make you want one more attempt? Is it visible enough to follow without hiding the next ring?

## Backyard Field Guide

Enable scanning, approach each of the five targets and look steadily at the actual object. Look away mid-scan and try scanning through a wall. Inspect the moving duck from different angles. A new entry should add one personal journal discovery, then remain available for the other player. Reload and check the journal remembers it.

Creative questions: Is scanning clear and calm while flying? Are the targets recognizable rather than invisible hotspots? Does the journal make the yard feel like a place to get to know?

## Finish with a quick fallback comparison

Compare v19 with v18b for flight feel, crawling, moving-human attachment, menu readability, retro comfort and obvious stutter. Report the build version, room player count, roles and activity when something goes wrong. Keep game-rule bugs separate from art feedback and headset performance observations. Neither a desktop automated pass nor this checklist proves Quest performance until you actually play it there.
