Built from v0.0.18b (5,443,933 bytes, 4bc06de6…e7834d). Keep v18b as the fallback. Same CDN dependencies as v18b (three.js r128, PeerJS 1.5.4). There are no new model files: all 17 embedded models are byte-identical. The new visuals (crumb, tag halo, ghost, paper moth, sign, journal icons) are drawn in code.

What's in it

A new Activities tab (first tab) in the headset menu and an Activities section in the page menu, each with four cards and big buttons. A wooden "ACTIVITIES" sign stands by one spawn can; it is visual only and has no collider. A single HUD line shows only the activity you are doing. Nothing turns on by itself when you join a room. Race points, Tag passes, crumb finds and journal entries are all kept separate.

Room coordinator: uses the same "race host" rule as racing (lowest player slot that is connected and still sending updates). Snapshots carry a version, room, epoch and revision. Requests are separate messages, credited to the player id the sending connection announced (not whatever the request claims), and each one is applied only once. When the coordinator leaves, the next player takes over with the epoch bumped, and old or out-of-order snapshots are ignored. This is peer-hosted with input checks, not cheat-proof.

Ways to check the code: window.__likeflies.act. The full module is in act19-embedded-module.js.

1. Buzz Tag

Implemented
  • Join and Leave. "Waiting for another fly" until a second player joins.
  • IT is chosen as whoever was IT least recently, then by stable id.
  • IT gets an amber pulsing ring and an IT badge; runners get a small blue marker. Both are separate objects, so they don't change the fly's size or collision.
  • The coordinator checks contact using the last 0.25 s of body-center positions as continuous paths, so a fast pass between updates still counts. Positions older than 0.5 s never count.
  • Tag reach = 0.55 × the smaller fly scale, about one body length (0.275 at the default size 0.5). Using the smaller fly means a bigger size slider never wins.
  • No tag through solid objects, using the game's own line-of-sight test.
  • One tag moves IT and gives the old IT +1 pass. The new IT has a 2 s get-ready window; the old IT is safe from them for 4 s; the same pair must separate past twice the reach before tagging again.
  • If IT becomes ineligible for more than 3 s, IT is handed on with no pass awarded.
  • Joining Tag stops your current race trial and pauses automatic race starts. Leaving Tag restores them.
  • Switching to Human leaves Tag. Leaving the room clears it.
Checked

Live, two then three desktop browsers on a local peer server: 12 of 12 checks passed (logs/tag19.log):

  • waiting alone
  • exactly one IT that everyone agrees on
  • tag transfer with +1 pass to the old IT
  • no ping-pong while overlapping for 5.5 s
  • a player not in Tag sitting on IT is never tagged
  • when the coordinator leaves, there is one agreed state (epoch up, passes kept, Tag paused with one member)
  • explicit Leave takes effect immediately

Single browser, with test position samples fed straight into the contact functions: a fast crossing is caught, a near miss is not, a teleport clears the history, an old position is ignored, and a house wall blocks the line of sight (logs/logic19.log).

Known limits
  • In the live test the contact was made by holding two flies together. A fast fly-through was missed because software-rendered browsers send updates only every 0.4 to 0.7 s; the crossing logic itself passed the single-browser check.
  • With three software browsers, updates got slow enough that contact never registered. Robin was therefore added after the Tag checks. Real headsets send 15 updates a second, but this is untested.
  • I raised the position age limit from 0.25 s to 0.5 s to cope with update jitter.
  • Not tested yet: death or role change during a live session, and the race restarting after leaving Tag. That test was written but never ran.
  • No room settings for Tag reach or timing (the defaults are fixed in code).
  • No direction arrow toward IT; the HUD shows IT's distance instead.

2. Hide-a-Crumb

Implemented
  • A golden folded crumb that is separate from food: it has no collider and no effect on food, health or physics.
  • Hiding uses the spot where you are currently landed. Press Hide here, see a preview, then Confirm or Cancel, with a choice of 8 stock hints.
  • Placement is checked: inside the yard, on a supporting surface, not inside anything, clear space for a fly to reach it, not on a moving thing (duck, chicken, grill lid, human), not next to a spawn can, not inside a race ring. If a spot fails you are told why.
  • One crumb per player, up to 8 per room. Only the owner can delete theirs.
  • The sparkle shows only to its owner, to someone who has found it, or to anyone within 4 m.
  • Seekers get a scent meter (Cold / Warm / HOT, with shapes as well as words) and a chirp when HOT. A stronger hint naming the nearest landmark appears after 60 s or when asked.
  • A find needs you within the find radius (0.5 + 0.6 × fly scale), with a clear view, for 0.4 s. The coordinator re-checks distance and visibility, records one find per seeker, and gives no reward for finding your own.
  • Export as a copyable code, or download as a JSON file. Import by pasting on desktop (limited to 4 KB, versioned, checked, placement re-validated). Imported crumbs never inherit the original creator's delete rights.
Checked

Live:

  • Job hides a crumb and both others receive it.
  • Job cannot find his own.
  • Sage finds it once; repeating the same request adds nothing.
  • The crumb survives Job leaving, and Robin then finds it, checked by the new coordinator.

Single browser:

  • A good lawn spot is accepted. Below ground, mid-air, outside the fence, invalid numbers and spawn-can spots are rejected.
  • Import works once with no duplicate. Codes are rejected for another yard layout, a bad spot, being oversized, garbage, the wrong format or invalid numbers, and names are stripped of markup.
  • Exporting gives a 209-character code.
Known limits
  • Room crumbs last only while someone stays in the room. Your own crumb and your finds are saved on your browser. Codes carry a hide to a later session; there are no cloud saves.
  • In the headset there is no paste; a friend already in the room can bring an imported crumb in for you.
  • Hiding needs you landed (there is no point-to-place).
  • The "nearest landmark" hint is a simple name lookup.

3. Ghost Wings

Implemented
  • Records about 5 position samples a second (time relative to the first gate) during finished non-practice laps: solo and ranked. Practice laps and laps that time out never replace a ghost.
  • Keeps the best per start ring and tuning, up to 6 ghosts. A cap of 3,600 samples and 128 KiB applies; a lap over that still counts, it just isn't saved as a ghost.
  • Options: Off, My best, or Record (the room's record).
  • Plays only from the same start ring and tuning, launching at the first gate, timed by the recording rather than frame rate. Shows the time gap at the same gate. Otherwise it says "No matching ghost for this start".
  • Watch plays a route once, labelled as a demonstration when it doesn't match your start.
  • The ghost is a cheap translucent cyan fly with a GHOST label. It has no collider and no score, is never a player and never takes part in Tag.
  • Sharing is opt-in (Share: off by default). It sends only the record holder's matching ghost on request, in chunks of 8 KB or less (20 at most), checks the sender and the data, and keeps at most 2 cached.
Checked

Single browser: a really flown lap saved a ghost (85 samples, 2.5 KB). A timed-out lap didn't replace it. The same start played it with a same-gate gap of +0.11 s; a different start said "No matching ghost". Invalid replays were rejected.

Live (6 of 6):

  • Sage's room record saved a ghost.
  • With sharing off, nothing was sent and Job saw "ghost not shared".
  • With sharing on, it arrived (2.5 KB) and played when Job raced from the same ring.
  • Off removed it.
  • Malformed, oversized, over-chunked and wrong-sender data was rejected. These bad chunks were fed in directly as a test.
Known limits
  • The ghost faces only left and right (yaw), with no pitch or roll.
  • Screenshots show the HUD gap line; the ghost itself is small and hard to spot in them.
  • Sharing needs the record holder in the room.

4. Backyard Field Guide

Implemented
  • Five entries built on real game objects:
    • the tallest sunflower head
    • the chicken
    • the live floating duck (follows its bobbing body)
    • the tomato plants
    • a new tiny paper moth stuck under a lawn chair seat (no collider)
  • Scan mode is a toggle. Looking at a target (within its own outline, within 14 m, with a clear view, menu closed) for 1.2 s fills a reticle; looking away drains it.
  • Journal with drawn icons and a one-line note per entry, saved on your browser. Discoveries are personal, never shared or used up by someone else.
Checked

Single browser: all 5 discovered by really aiming the camera. Looking away made no progress. Looking at the chicken from behind something solid didn't scan. The journal, ghost, my crumb and imports survived a reload. The duck target moves 1.8 m in 2.5 s; looking at the pool water does not scan the duck (logs/duck19.log).

Known limits
  • Scanning works for flies only.
  • Scans 10 times a second against 5 targets.
  • The moth spot was found by a probe under the chair; a fallback position exists if the probe fails.
  • No discovery message to other players.

Regression and environment

  • Passed: the code passes a syntax check. All 17 models are identical. v18b's fly legs and the one-time defaults migration are still present, and the defaults load. Racing laps, ranked records and shared tuning publishing worked during the ghost tests. Menus render on desktop and on the headset panel canvas: the tabs now size to their labels, and the card text wraps.
  • NOT RUN (interrupted before starting):
    • the role-change / race-restart check
    • the v16 single-player race suite
    • food, swatting and human-hand regressions (not checked this round)
  • All testing was desktop Chromium with software rendering, on a local static server and a local peer server. Live means real peer connections between browsers in one machine. No headset testing and no Quest frame rate.
  • The v18b known limits still apply (contact approximations, the tuning host rule, rotated race starts).

10 to 15 minute headset script (Job and Sage)

  1. Both join the same room. Open the menu: the first tab is Activities.
  2. Buzz Tag: both press Join Buzz Tag. One of you gets the amber ring and IT badge. Chase each other, both through the air and crawling under the picnic bench. After a tag, check the 2 s get-ready countdown and the 4 s safe window. Then one of you leaves.
  3. Hide-a-Crumb: Job lands somewhere sneaky, presses Hide here, picks a hint, and confirms. Sage presses Next crumb until he finds Job's, then Find this, and follows Cold, Warm, HOT. Swap roles.
  4. Ghost Wings: fly one full race lap. Set My best, then start again from the same ring and race your ghost. Sage sets Share: on, and Job picks Record.
  5. Field Guide: Scan: ON. Find the sunflower, chicken, duck, tomatoes, and the secret under a lawn chair. Check the journal.
  6. Tell us: is Tag fun with two players? Is the reach too small or too big? Is anything confusing? How are the frame rate and comfort?
